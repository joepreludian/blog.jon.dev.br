I am wrapping up another week, preparing my notes for the next one and sketching out my routine here. I stopped for a moment to reflect on a recent project I generated and reached an interesting conclusion: generated code can be read as the output of a project printed on a 3d printer.

Let me walk through the reflection. Picture a filament 3d printer. You download a model, adjust the parameters, print. At first you fail a lot, you take the printer apart, you understand how it works and you start tuning the parts until you can produce artifacts.

With time you mature the tool, you learn to lay down the base layers, you learn printing techniques. Success.

There are also the "makers" who only grab ready-made parts. They do not model anything; they just print, discard the pieces, or send them back to be melted down and turned into filament again. There are those who print pile after pile of fidget toys — things that lose their appeal right after printing and end up forgotten. None of it serves to learn, to refine technique, or to be useful in any way.

And there are those who work professionally, who create simple, functional parts with a practical application. The professional who can keep things consistent and print large pieces by simply breaking the model into parts that can be printed and assembled coherently. These pieces carry far more value than the small ones, solving everyday problems.

For this second group of professionals, the 3d printer can be an unlimited source of resources. They treat the filament printer as a canvas where they can create whatever part they want.

This professional learns how to model in 3d. They understand the implications and how to solve the everyday problems of printing. How to produce quality work at high resolution, respecting the limits of the equipment, the filament and the 3d modeling software. They understand how to assemble complex structures. They learn about material physics. They get into static force calculations and how to build efficient truss schemes, or load-bearing structures.

My whole point is: the most basic practitioner stops at the basics. They rely on pre-rendered models, focus on printing and generate nothing relevant. They may even learn to operate the printing tool, but without context, without a goal, things become useless.

I say this because I see a lot of AI-generated content that is simply good for nothing. Built for a simple application, but one that does not scale. However intelligent the frontier models have become, not knowing how to ask for things keeps the repertoire of whoever is asking limited. On top of that, the bar also drops, so any kind of code gets accepted.

For a long time now engineers have been bombarded with claims that their jobs are at risk, that a great revolution is about to erupt and that it is only a matter of time before the work is replaced. Well, LLMs are available to everyone. Why do we not see crowds of rich people, with plenty of quality software shipped, prospering and making a lot of money without knowing the technical side?

Of course AIs arrived to amplify people's work, but it is still very much tied to what the person knows (or understands) how to do. That is why a project generated by an engineer versus one by a non-technical person shows glaring differences in quality — using the very same coding agent.

I believe everything revolves around how well you understand what is being asked. The quality of your thinking reflects directly in the quality of your code: a good architecture requires prior study of the technologies, the limitations and, above all, experience of what works and what does not. Understanding that working code is not always the best alternative. AI wants to solve the problem; if you do not give it the constraints, any answer will do.

Bad codebases with AI are just bad codebases generated with more bad codebase. Thinking about the architecture and a project pattern is the responsibility of a software architect. A junior with AI is not a senior, but a junior with more chances of making mistakes.

Well, that is the reflection. What do you think about it? I would really like to hear your thoughts.
